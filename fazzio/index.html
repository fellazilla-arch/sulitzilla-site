How this works

  1. Sign the agreement. Read it at the bottom of this page, then sign online with the private link the seller sends you.
  2. Handover. You pay the ₱4,000 down payment and take the motorcycle. From then on, this page shows your due dates.
  3. Every month. Send ₱3,000 to the seller by the due date. Once it’s received, that payment turns green (“Paid”) below. Up to 5 days late, there’s no fee.
  4. Extra money. Whenever you have extra, send it to the seller. Once it’s recorded, it shows up on this page: your balance and payoff amount go down, and your last payments are crossed off.
  5. Need a break? Tell the seller and hand back the motorcycle, as long as you’re no more than one payment behind. Payments pause for up to 6 months with no fees or interest, everything you’ve paid stays credited, and your due dates move back.
  6. Last payment. Within 7 days you get the signed Deed of Absolute Sale and the original OR/CR, and you handle the transfer.

Payments are marked here by the seller when they’re received, so a new payment may take a little while to show.

If a payment is late

Life happens, so there’s a grace period. A message before the due date is always appreciated.

    Main rules at a glance

    • Ownership. The motorcycle and its OR/CR stay in the seller’s name until the last payment. Then it’s yours, and the transfer fees are yours (clauses 6 and 13).
    • While you have it. You pay for maintenance, registration renewal and the required CTPL insurance. You’re responsible for traffic violations, camera (NCAP) tickets and accidents (clauses 7 to 9).
    • Not allowed until fully paid. Selling, pawning, renting out, lending it out for long periods, using it as collateral, or major modifications (clause 7).
    • Paying early. Pay extra or pay off anytime, with no penalty. You only pay the principal still owed (clause 4).
    • Pausing. You can pause anytime, as often as you need, by handing back the motorcycle, for up to 6 months each time, as long as you’re no more than one payment behind. Any overdue payment is paid with your first payment after resuming. The seller holds it at his own risk. If registration or CTPL comes due during a pause, the seller pays it and you repay it with your first payment after resuming. If you don’t resume within 6 months, the seller may sell the motorcycle and keep what has been paid (clause 12).
    • Falling behind. If two payments are overdue, you get a written notice and 15 days to catch up. If you don’t, the seller may cancel the agreement: you return the motorcycle within 7 days, and what you’ve paid is kept as rent for its use (clauses 10 and 11).
