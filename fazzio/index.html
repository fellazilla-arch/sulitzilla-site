      Proposed terms

      What you agree to when you take the motorcycle home

      These are the proposed terms for discussion. The final written agreement will be reviewed for compliance with Philippine law (including the Civil Code rules on installment sales and LTO registration and transfer requirements) and will govern if anything differs from this page.

      Ownership
      • The Seller keeps legal ownership until every installment is paid, subject to the final legally reviewed ownership and financing structure.
      • The Certificate of Registration (CR) stays in the Seller’s name during the installment period. The Seller keeps the original CR.
      • The Buyer receives possession of and responsibility for the motorcycle from handover. The Seller provides the current Official Receipt (OR), a copy of the CR, and a signed authorization to use the motorcycle so the Buyer can ride legally.
      • The Buyer may not sell, transfer, pawn, lease, mortgage, or otherwise dispose of the motorcycle without the Seller’s written permission.
      • When the full installment price is paid, the Seller will sign a Deed of Sale and hand over the original CR and the documents needed to transfer ownership with the LTO.
      • Transfer expenses (LTO fees, any required clearance, emission test, notarization) are the Buyer’s responsibility.
      Registration and insurance

      The Buyer is responsible for:

      • Annual registration renewal, on time.
      • Compulsory Third-Party Liability (CTPL) insurance.
      • Any comprehensive insurance agreed with the Seller.
      • All registration-related expenses.
      • Keeping valid insurance throughout the installment period.

      Recommended: comprehensive insurance with theft coverage, naming the Seller as loss payee until the plan is paid. Without it, the Buyer still owes the remaining balance if the motorcycle is stolen or written off.

      Maintenance and repairs

      The Buyer is responsible for:

      • Oil changes and routine servicing.
      • Tire, battery, and brake pad replacement.
      • Normal wear and tear.
      • Repairs resulting from accidents, misuse, or negligence.

      Pre-existing mechanical defects disclosed or discovered after delivery will be handled according to the final written agreement. The motorcycle’s condition will be documented with photos and the odometer reading at handover.

      Accidents and damage

      The Buyer must:

      • Tell the Seller immediately about any accident.
      • Tell the Seller immediately if the motorcycle is stolen or impounded.
      • Keep the motorcycle in reasonable condition.
      • Pay for damage resulting from his use, subject to applicable insurance coverage.
      Usage restrictions
      • No modifications without the Seller’s approval.
      • No sale or transfer without written permission.
      • No use for illegal activities.
      • No taking the motorcycle outside the Philippines without permission.
      • No letting another person become the main rider without approval.

      Default and early repayment

      What happens if things go wrong — and if you pay early

      Paying early (encouraged)
      • You can pay off the whole balance at any time with no penalty.
      • The early pay-off amount is the remaining share of the cash price (shown in the schedule’s “Pay-off amount” column) plus any unpaid late fees. The unused financing charge is waived.
      • Extra payments of any size are welcome. They are applied to the balance and shorten the plan, and the remaining financing charge is reduced in the same proportion.
      • Every payment is split in a fixed proportion between the cash price and the financing charge, so you can always see how much of the cash price you still owe.
      How the outstanding balance is calculated
      • Remaining balance = the scheduled installments not yet paid, plus any unpaid late fees.
      • Early pay-off amount = the remaining share of the cash price plus any unpaid late fees (unused financing charge waived).
      • The Seller will give a written statement of the balance within 3 days of any request.
      Written notices
      • Notices are sent by text message or Messenger and by email or a signed letter to the address in the final agreement.
      • A notice sent electronically counts as received on the day it is sent. Either party may update their contact details in writing.
      What counts as default
      • consecutive missed installments trigger a formal default notice.
      • Default occurs if, after that notice, the overdue amount is not paid within the cure period, or a total of installments are unpaid.
      • Selling, pawning, transferring, or hiding the motorcycle without permission is also default.
      • Letting the registration or required insurance lapse, and not fixing it within 15 days of a written notice, is also default.
      Your chance to fix a default
      • After a formal default notice you have days to pay the overdue installments and late fees. Doing so restores the plan to good standing as if nothing happened.
      • The Seller may also agree to a revised schedule in writing. Asking for one early is always better than missing payments.
      Remedies if a default isn’t fixed
      • The Seller may only use the remedies allowed by law for installment sales of personal property (Civil Code, Art. 1484). These are: requiring payment of the overdue amounts; cancelling the sale if two or more installments are unpaid; or foreclosing a chattel mortgage if one was set up.
      • If the Seller takes back the motorcycle by cancelling the sale or foreclosing, the Seller cannot also go after the Buyer for the rest of the unpaid balance.
      • The motorcycle will only be recovered through voluntary surrender or proper legal process — never by force or without notice.
      • Payments already made are not automatically forfeited. Any settlement follows the final agreement and applicable law.
      Returning the motorcycle voluntarily
      • If the plan no longer works for you, you may return the motorcycle by written notice at any time. You will not be treated as in default if you raise it early.
      • The motorcycle is inspected together and valued at fair market value (agreed by both parties, or the average of two independent dealer quotes), less the cost of any damage beyond normal wear.
      • If that value is more than your early pay-off amount plus unpaid late fees, the Seller refunds you the difference after selling the motorcycle. If it is less, the Seller absorbs the shortfall.
